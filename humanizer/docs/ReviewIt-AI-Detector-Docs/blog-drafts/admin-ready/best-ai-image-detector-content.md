## What is an AI image detector, and who needs one?

An AI image detector is a tool that looks at a picture and tells you whether it appears to have been generated by AI or taken as an original photograph. People use one for many reasons:

- **Students** check images used in coursework, where an assignment may ask for original photographs.
- **Researchers and academics** want to be sure that an image presented as an observation is a real one.
- **Editors, bloggers and journalists** need to check a picture before they publish or share it.
- **Designers and marketers** want to know what they are working with.
- **Anyone who shares pictures online** and does not want to pass on something misleading.

In every case, the question is the same: can I trust this image? The best AI image detector helps you answer it honestly.

## What makes the best AI image detector? Six things to look for

### 1. A clear verdict, not just a number

A percentage such as "63% likely AI" leaves you guessing where the line is. The best AI image detector gives you a plain verdict you can read at a glance, so you know straight away what the result means.

### 2. Honesty about uncertainty

Some images are simply hard to judge. A tool that forces a confident answer every time will sometimes be confidently wrong. The best AI image detector is willing to say that a result is not conclusive, and to tell you which way it leans.

### 3. Accuracy you can understand

Every tool claims to be accurate. Look for a clear figure, a clear statement of what it applies to, and an honest reminder that no detector is perfect.

### 4. Support for the files you really use

You should not have to convert a picture before you can check it. Look for support for the common image formats, and a generous file size limit.

### 5. Privacy

You may be checking sensitive or unpublished images. Find out whether the tool keeps them, and for how long.

### 6. A sensible next step

A result is only the beginning. The best AI image detector reminds you that it is one signal among several, and helps you combine it with your own checks.

## Where it has limits

An honest guide should say where a tool falls short.

- **No detector is perfect.** A result is a signal, not proof. A real photograph that has been heavily edited, filtered or compressed can look different from an untouched one, and generated pictures can be edited to look more natural.
- **The in-between verdicts are not conclusive.** "More towards AI" and "More towards Original" are hints about which way the image leans. They are not answers.
- **Small or low-quality images are harder.** The less detail a picture has, the less there is to examine.
- **It does not replace your own checks.** Finding the source, searching for earlier copies and checking the context still matter.
- **Follow the rules that apply to you.** If your course, publisher or workplace has a policy on AI images, it still applies.
