## What are AI humanizer styles?

An AI humanizer style is a tone setting for a rewrite. You choose a style, and the Humanizer rewrites the sentence you selected so that it reads in that tone while keeping your meaning. Your citations, numbers and names stay protected, so the facts stay yours.

Humoro's [AI Humanizer](https://humoro.io/humanizer) has six styles: Academic, Business, Creative, Casual, Narrative and Technical. You rewrite only the sentences you choose, so a style never touches the parts of your paper you are happy with.

## The six styles at a glance

- **Academic.** Scholarly, precise and formal. For essays, theses, research papers and literature reviews.
- **Business.** Direct, clear and professional, without corporate jargon. For reports, proposals, memos and emails.
- **Creative.** Engaging and vivid, with natural phrasing. For personal statements, reflective writing and creative pieces.
- **Casual.** Warm, friendly and relaxed. For newsletters, blog posts and informal writing.
- **Narrative.** Storytelling cadence with smooth transitions. For long-form essays, case studies and any piece that tells a story.
- **Technical.** Clean, concrete and straightforward terminology. For documentation, methods sections and engineering or science writing.

## Academic: for essays, theses and research papers

The Academic style keeps the scholarly tone that most university work expects. It aims for precise, formal wording that is still easy to read, and it respects the parts of a paper that must not change, such as citations and reference entries.

Use it for a literature review, an argument paragraph, a methodology section or a discussion. If you are unsure which style to pick for coursework, this is the safe default.

Watch for one thing: formal does not mean wordy. If a rewritten sentence feels heavy, read it aloud and trim it. The University of Manchester's [Academic Phrasebank](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/) is a useful free reference for natural academic phrasing.

## Business: for reports, proposals and memos

The Business style is direct and professional. It aims to remove corporate filler and vague buzzwords so the point comes first. That makes it a good fit for a report summary, a project proposal, a cover note or a workplace email.

Use it when your reader is busy and wants the answer quickly. Purdue OWL's [general guidelines for business writing](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/professional_technical_writing/business_writing_for_administrative_and_clerical_staff/general_guidelines.html) explain why a formal but confident tone works in that setting.

Watch for one thing: business writing is not the same as academic writing. If you use it in an essay, the tone may read as too brisk.

## Creative: for personal and vivid writing

The Creative style favours engaging, vivid phrasing. It suits personal statements, reflective journals, application essays and any piece where your voice and imagery matter more than formality.

Use it when a sentence feels flat and you want it to sound like a person with something to say. Keep it away from sections that need strict precision, such as results or definitions.

## Casual: for blogs, newsletters and informal pieces

The Casual style is warm, friendly and relaxed. It is the right choice for a blog post, a newsletter, a social media caption or a class discussion post, where a stiff tone would push readers away.

Watch for one thing: casual wording can feel out of place in graded academic work. Check your assignment brief before you choose it. Purdue OWL's guide to [levels of formality](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/using_appropriate_language/levels_of_formality.html) helps you decide how formal a piece needs to be.

## Narrative: for storytelling and long-form essays

The Narrative style gives sentences a storytelling cadence, with smooth transitions from one idea to the next. It suits long-form essays, case studies, profiles and any writing that walks the reader through a sequence of events.

Use it for the paragraphs that tell what happened, such as the background of a case study or the story behind an argument. For analysis and evidence, switch back to Academic or Technical.

## Technical: for documentation and methods

The Technical style prefers clean, concrete terminology and straightforward sentences. It suits documentation, methods sections, lab reports and engineering or science writing, where clarity matters more than flourish.

Use it when a sentence is correct but awkward, for example a long, tangled description of a procedure. Always check that technical terms, units and formulas are exactly as you intended.

## How do you choose the right style?

Ask three quick questions about the sentence you are rewriting.

1. **Who is reading it?** A marker, a manager, a customer or a friend. The reader sets the tone.
2. **What is the sentence doing?** Explaining a method, making an argument, telling a story or persuading.
3. **What does the assignment brief say?** If it asks for formal or academic writing, start with Academic or Technical.

Here is a simple guide.

- Essay, thesis or research paper: **Academic**.
- Lab report, methods or documentation: **Technical**.
- Report, proposal or work email: **Business**.
- Case study or long-form essay that tells a story: **Narrative**.
- Personal statement or reflective piece: **Creative**.
- Blog post, newsletter or informal post: **Casual**.

## Can you use more than one style in the same document?

Yes. You choose a style for each sentence you rewrite, so one document can use several. A paper might use Technical for a methods sentence, Academic for the argument and Narrative for a short case example. Use that freedom carefully: too many changes of tone in one section can make the writing feel uneven.

## Tips for getting the best result

- **Match the style to the sentence, not the whole paper.** A methods sentence and a reflective sentence rarely need the same tone.
- **Rewrite less, not more.** Choose only the sentences that need work. The rest of your paper already sounds like you.
- **Read every rewrite.** A style changes tone, not your ideas. Make sure the meaning is still yours.
- **Keep what only you can say.** Specific examples, your own data and your own views are what make writing sound personal.
- **Check your facts and references.** Citations, numbers and names are protected, but you should still confirm them.

## Where styles have limits

An honest guide should say what a style cannot do.

- **A style changes tone, not facts.** It will not add evidence or correct a wrong claim.
- **No style can promise a result in any detector.** Detectors differ, and no tool can predict another tool's score.
- **A style does not replace your own review.** You are responsible for the final text.
