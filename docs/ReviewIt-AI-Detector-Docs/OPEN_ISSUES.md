| B2 | *new* — A click takes 10–35 s on this PC | In the 29 Oct "Humanize all" run (26 sentences, one after the other) a click took 4–35 s, mean 12.7 s. | B4 | *new* — Some sentences do not reach 30% with any Gemini rewrite | Live, per spelled-out candidate over two runs: the Kumasi sentence (names, acronym, list of three, place) 0 of 24 over three runs; "Nursing leadership succession planning…" 0 of 8; the data-collection sentence 0 of 11; the reflexive-analysis sentence 1 of 11; against 5 of 6 for the sub-Saharan sentence and 3 of 3 for "Existing informal mechanisms…". Offline, no hand-written rewrite of the Kumasi sentence reached 30% (0 of 15). Whether a sentence passes depends more on the sentence than on the wording of the prompt. No prompt change so far brings these sentences below 30%; fixing them is the main open prompt problem (the target is 90% of flagged sentences). |
| B5 | *new* — Results differ a lot from run to run | The same 8 sentences gave 8 of 8 and 5 of 8 at 30% or below; a sentence that failed its first call on one run passed on the next. First-call passes on the first 8 sentences: 2, 3 and 3 of 8 in three runs with the old first call, 3 of 8 and 6 of 8 with the new one (the second of those runs has 8 slightly different sentences). Any change needs at least two runs on the same sentences before it is judged; report a range. Not yet known: whether the 2 Nov first-call change (2 ordinary + 3 spelled-out) beats the earlier first call. |
| B6 | *new* — Spelled-out rewrites can drift in meaning | Checked by hand on 5 retry rewrites: 3 faithful, 1 with a statement that became separate claims plus an added "current", 1 borderline (a new standalone statement about the region). The prompt now asks for faithfulness; the guard cannot judge it; before the evening of 2 Nov the lowest score won, so a drifted rewrite could be the one shown (example: "stakeholders who work across three models", where the original says they were recruited across them). **Since the evening of 2 Nov:** among Gemini's rewrites at 30% or below the one closest to the original's length is shown first (the lowest score breaks a tie), so a longer rewrite no longer beats a short faithful one that also passes; it cannot help when no short rewrite passes. |
| B7 | *new* — The 90% target is measured on one detector with each rewrite scored alone | Every number in the 2 Nov reports is the local BERT detector's score of one rewrite on its own, while the detector scores blocks of 4 sentences and only one detector is used (not ZeroGPT, Turnitin or Copyleaks). The humanized document has not been re-scored with the current prompts, and a one-word change flipped scores offline, so the results may not carry over to another detector. What the 90% means and how it is measured is not written down. |
| B8 | *new* — Spelled-out rewrites make the text longer | Winners were often 1.6–2× the original's length (the guard allows up to 2.2×), so a whole paper would grow and some phrasing is padded. Since the evening of 2 Nov a short rewrite wins when it also passes, but when none does a long one is shown. The per-candidate data shows no gain beyond about 1.5× (`RETRY_AND_FIRST_CALL_EXPERIMENTS_2026-11-02.md`); a lower cap was not applied. |
